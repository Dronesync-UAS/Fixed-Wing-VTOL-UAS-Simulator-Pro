# The departure spiral: a feedback loop through the airframe

12 October 2026

## What was reported

> "i also noticed that aircraft becomes very anstable when transiting to
> hover mode to forward flying. the aircraft went cracy and not is flying
> upside down."

A screenshot taken during the same flight shows BANK 41 degrees in
fixed wing at 78 m, 696 m from home.

## The cause

`forward_flight.py`'s last step, `step_climb_to_cruise`, was rewritten
earlier the same day to stop the aircraft loitering at a single waypoint
while it climbed. The rewrite re-sent `MAV_CMD_DO_REPOSITION` roughly
once a second for up to three minutes, each time aiming 420 m ahead **on
the aircraft's current heading**.

Every `DO_REPOSITION` sets a loiter point. PX4 v1.15.4,
`navigator_main.cpp` line 291:

```
rep->current.type = position_setpoint_s::SETPOINT_TYPE_LOITER;
```

So the loiter centre was being moved, once a second, to wherever the nose
happened to be pointing. That closes a loop through the airframe:

    small bank -> heading changes -> target swings further that way
                -> larger bank commanded -> heading changes faster -> ...

On a multicopter this would wander. On a fixed wing, which banks to turn,
it winds up into a tightening spiral. 41 degrees of bank is that loop
part-way round.

The same mechanism explains "the aircraft jumps when height is changed":
`climb_to.py` correctly uses `MAV_CMD_DO_CHANGE_ALTITUDE`, which leaves
the horizontal setpoint alone - but if a forward-flight sequence was
still running in the background, its repositions were fighting the climb.

## The fix

Take the bearing ONCE, at the start of the climb-out leg, and hold it.
The target is then a fixed direction in the world rather than a direction
relative to the aircraft, and there is no loop to close.

The leg is re-extended only when the aircraft has come within 150 m of
the point it was sent to, always along that same held bearing, at most
eight times. Between extensions nothing is commanded at all: PX4 is left
to fly the leg it was given.

A second fault surfaced while writing the test for the first: the step
commanded its opening leg unconditionally, so pressing FORWARD FLIGHT
while already near the range limit sent the aircraft further out. It now
holds instead.

## What the test now says

`tools/test_recovery.py` previously REQUIRED the broken behaviour - "kept
re-aiming the leg so the climb stays straight (>= 2 legs)". A test that
encodes a bug protects it. It now:

* requires exactly ONE leg for an ordinary climb;
* swings the nose 200 degrees across one run while the aircraft keeps
  eating into its leg, and requires every commanded target to stay on the
  same meridian - zero sideways drift - with each one further along;
* requires no outbound leg at all when the aircraft starts the step near
  the range limit.

The middle one is the check that would have caught this before it flew.

## Where it stands

The diagnosis is read off PX4 v1.15.4's own navigator source, and the
reported symptom matches it. The FIX HAS NOT BEEN FLOWN. The next
departure is the test of it, and the thing to watch is the bank angle in
the thirty seconds after the transition completes: it should settle, not
build.
