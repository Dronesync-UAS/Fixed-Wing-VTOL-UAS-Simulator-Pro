# How a real VTOL transitions, and what the station was doing instead

12 October 2026

> "major issues when transiting from hover to forward flying. what is the
> realistic process to transit either way in real flights"

## The real procedure

### Forward transition - hover to wing-borne

1. **Climb to a safe transition height in multirotor.** Enough to
   recover if the transition fails. Small UAS practice is tens of
   metres, not a few.
2. **Point into wind, wings level, not turning.** Headwind means more
   airspeed for less ground speed, so the transition finishes sooner and
   in less distance.
3. **Pusher spools up while the lift motors hold height.** The aircraft
   accelerates roughly level - it does not climb through this.
4. **The wing progressively takes the load** as airspeed builds, and the
   lift motors unload.
5. **At transition airspeed the lift motors stop** and the aircraft is
   wing-borne.
6. **Only then** does it climb away, accelerate, and turn.

### Back transition - wing-borne to hover

1. **Pusher to idle, decelerate**, wings level.
2. **Lift motors spin up** while there is still airspeed.
3. **Speed falls below transition airspeed**, the wing unloads and the
   rotors take the weight.
4. It needs DISTANCE - a back transition is not a stop. Space has to be
   planned for it.

### The rule that matters most

**Straight and level through the whole thing, both ways.** During a
transition the wing is only part-loaded and the lift motors are
unloading. A bank in that window is a bank with very little holding it
up. Real crews do not turn through a transition and do not transition
low.

## PX4 v1.15.4's own figures

From `src/modules/vtol_att_control/vtol_att_control_params.c`:

| Parameter | Default | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `VT_F_TRANS_DUR` | 5.0 s | How long the forward transition takes |
| `VT_ARSP_BLEND` | 8.0 m/s | Airspeed at which MC and FW controls start blending |
| `VT_ARSP_TRANS` | 10.0 m/s | Airspeed at which it switches to fixed wing |
| `VT_TRANS_TIMEOUT` | 15.0 s | Transition cancelled after this |
| `VT_F_TRANS_THR` | 1.0 | Throttle target during the forward transition |
| `VT_B_TRANS_DUR` | 10.0 s | Maximum back transition duration |
| `VT_B_DEC_MSS` | 2.0 m/s² | Deceleration assumed for back transition distance |

The figure that explains the trouble is `VT_ARSP_TRANS = 10 m/s`. PX4
declares the transition **over** at 10 m/s. That is wing-borne, but
barely - and the station was commanding a turn the instant PX4 said
"fixed wing".

## What the station was doing wrong

Three things, all of them the opposite of the procedure above:

1. **It could transition in a turn.** Nothing checked bank angle. PX4
   will not refuse it.
2. **It turned immediately on reaching fixed wing**, at around 10 m/s,
   before the aircraft had accelerated on the wing.
3. **It then re-aimed that turn on the aircraft's own heading once a
   second** - the feedback loop written up in the previous entry, which
   wound the result into a spiral.

Item 3 was fixed first because it is the one that inverted the aircraft.
Items 1 and 2 are fixed here.

## The fix

Two steps added to the departure sequence, which is now seven steps:

* **`step_wings_level`**, before the transition. Waits for roll under 30
  degrees, up to eleven seconds. Commands nothing while it waits.
* **`step_settle`**, after it. Waits for airspeed to reach 16 m/s - a
  margin over PX4's 10 m/s `VT_ARSP_TRANS` - before anything may turn,
  up to twenty seconds. If there is no airspeed reading it holds
  straight for six seconds instead. Commands nothing while it waits.

Both are checked in `tools/test_recovery.py` for what they wait on and
for commanding nothing while they wait, not merely for existing.

## Where it stands

The procedure above is standard VTOL practice and the PX4 figures are
read from v1.15.4's own parameter file. The station's two new steps have
NOT been flown. The back transition (HOVER from fixed wing) has not been
reviewed yet - it needs the same treatment, and it needs the
deceleration distance thinking about, because a back transition commanded
close to the range limit will cross it.
