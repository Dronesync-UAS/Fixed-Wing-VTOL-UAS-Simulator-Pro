# Autonomous mission planning

12 October 2026

> "Generate a autonomous mission planning qgroundcontrol stile with the
> different types of surveys dn operations including gimbals camera
> activation (research qground control)"

## What was built

Three files, split so that the part worth checking can be checked with
no simulator running:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `mission.py` | the patterns and the arithmetic. No pymavlink, no network, no PX4. |
| `mission_upload.py` | the MAVLink mission protocol. Needs a live link. |
| `plugins/mission_plan.py` | the control in the console: plan, upload, start. |

## The patterns, and what each is for in training

| Pattern | Use |
|---|---|
| **Survey** (lawnmower) | mapping an area. The bread-and-butter sortie a student must be able to plan. |
| **Corridor** | following a line - pipeline, railway, river, a road after flooding. |
| **Orbit** | circling a point with the camera held on it. Structure inspection, incident overwatch, and the exercise that shows what a gimbal is for. |
| **Perimeter** | flying a boundary rather than filling it. Fence-line search, patrol. |
| **Waypoints** | a list of points, flown in order. |

Corridor and waypoints need a path drawn on the map, which the console
cannot do yet. The control says so rather than pretending.

## The numbers are worked out, not typed

This is the part that matters. Line spacing and camera trigger distance
come from the camera's field of view, the height above ground and the
overlap figures - the standard photogrammetry relations:

```
frame width on the ground  = 2 * height * tan(hfov / 2)
line spacing               = frame width * (1 - side overlap)
frame length on the ground = 2 * height * tan(vfov / 2)
trigger distance           = frame length * (1 - front overlap)
```

The vertical field of view is DERIVED from the horizontal one and the
image's aspect ratio, so the two cannot disagree when the image size
changes.

With the payload camera as `tools/gimbal_apply.py` builds it - 1.05 rad
horizontal, 1024x576 - at 100 m with the usual 70% front and 60% side
overlap:

| | |
|---|---|
| frame on the ground | 115.8 m across, 65.2 m along |
| line spacing | 46.3 m |
| camera every | 19.5 m |

Those are the figures students get wrong by hand, and getting them wrong
means a survey with gaps that nobody notices until the photographs are
processed.

## The gimbal is part of the plan

A survey points the camera straight down (`DO_MOUNT_CONTROL`, pitch
-90) and triggers it by distance (`DO_SET_CAM_TRIGG_DIST`). An orbit
instead holds the camera on the thing being orbited
(`DO_SET_ROI_LOCATION`), which is what makes an orbit an inspection
rather than a circle. Both are released at the end.

## Every mission has the same shape

1. `NAV_VTOL_TAKEOFF` - not `NAV_TAKEOFF`, or a VTOL takes off as a
   fixed wing with nothing underneath it.
2. Transition to fixed wing, if the pattern is to be flown on the wing.
   An orbit at 120 m is better as a multirotor; a survey covering half a
   kilometre is not.
3. Point the gimbal, start the camera.
4. The pattern.
5. Stop the camera, release the gimbal.
6. Back to multirotor, then return to launch.

## Three presses, not one

Plan, then upload, then start. A mission that uploads and launches on a
single click is a mission nobody has read. Planning reports how far, how
long, how many frames, the line spacing and the trigger distance - and
says plainly that the time is a straight-line estimate which does not
count the turn at the end of each line.

## MAVLink details that bite

* **Item 0 is home.** PX4 treats the first item as home and does not fly
  to it. `mission.py` does not know that; `mission_upload.py` adds it,
  in the one place already handling the protocol.
* **The upload is a conversation.** PX4 asks for the items it wants, in
  whatever order, and may ask twice. The loop answers requests until the
  ack arrives rather than sending the list and hoping.
* **Latitude and longitude are integers** in `MISSION_ITEM_INT` -
  degrees times 1e7. Floats there put the mission off the coast of
  Africa.

Command numbers were read from MAVLink's own `common.xml`, not recalled:
16, 19, 20, 84, 85, 178, 195, 197, 205, 206, 2000, 2001, 3000.

## Where it stands

`tools/test_mission.py`, 55 checks: the photogrammetry relations worked
by hand as well as in the module, every pattern measured (line lengths,
spacing, orbit radius, a closed perimeter, a corridor that straddles its
path), the order of a built mission, the command numbers against the
definitions, and the uploader's own rules read out of its source.

NOT FLOWN. Nothing has been uploaded to a running PX4. The console's
Mission tab exists and all its fields are checked present in the browser
test, but no mission has been flown from it.

Still to do: drawing a survey area or a corridor path ON THE MAP, which
is what QGroundControl's Plan view really is. At the moment the pattern
is built around wherever the aircraft happens to be, with a size and a
bearing typed in. That is enough to fly a survey; it is not enough to
plan one over a particular field.
