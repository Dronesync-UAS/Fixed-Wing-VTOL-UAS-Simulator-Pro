# Two operating envelopes: CAA 500 m, and 100 km for instructors

12 October 2026

## What was asked

> "lets have two options CAA compliant 500m max and a training option only
> for instructors of 100km"

This followed a question about whether the satellite imagery runs out on a
long flight. It does, but it is not the first thing that does.

## What actually ran out, in order

Measured, not assumed:

| What | Reach from home | Where it is set |
|---|---|---|
| Station range limit | 500 m | `LIMITS["range_m"]` |
| Satellite backdrop | 2 km | `HALF_EXTENT_M` in the fetcher |
| Visible ground, plane worlds | 4 km | `<plane><size>8000 8000` |
| Visible ground, heightmap worlds | 2 km (Hoggar) to 8 km (Black Rock) | each world's `<heightmap><size>` |
| Ground collision | 50 km | `ground_collision` box, 100 km square |

So the imagery was the second limit, not the first, and the ground
collision - the one nobody had noticed - was the last.

## Decisions

### A named profile, not an editable number

The 500 m figure is read by the breach warnings, the altitude tape, the
limit bars, the event log and the flight log. Letting an instructor retype
it would leave a training record that cannot answer "was this flown to the
CAA limit on purpose?".

`range_profile.py` holds two named envelopes. Switching one logs an
instructor action, and every flight log written afterwards carries the
envelope's name and whether it is the student-safe one - not just its
numbers. `flight_log.py` reads its three limit keys by name, so the extra
`profile`, `profile_label` and `student_safe` keys ride along without it
needing to change.

### Only the range changes

The ceiling stays at 120 m and the speed limit at 30 m/s in both
envelopes. The request was about distance. Raising the height limit
alongside it would mean a student could be flown outside the height rule
without anyone having chosen that. If the extended envelope should also
lift the ceiling, that is one line in `range_profile.py` and it should be
a decision somebody takes on purpose.

### The control is in Settings, not on a rail

It is not a flying command. It is set once at the top of a lesson and
changes what every warning means for the rest of it - which is worth two
clicks and a panel, not one click next to LAND.

### The plugin is NOT called `range_profile.py`

`plugins/operating_envelope.py` imports the top-level `range_profile`.
Naming both the same was tried first and produced a circular import the
moment `plugins/` was on the path, because the plugin loader names each
module after its own filename.

### Limits now travel in telemetry

The page used to be rendered with whichever envelope was active at the
time, substituted into its JavaScript. Switching mid-session would have
left the limit bars and the altitude tape reading the old figures until
somebody reloaded. `/telemetry` now carries `limits` every poll and the
page prefers it over the baked-in copy.

### Imagery: the zoom falls as the extent grows

Area goes up as the square of the distance. 100 km at zoom 16 is about
22,000 tiles and a picture nothing can open. `--extent-km` therefore drops
a zoom level at a time until the download is inside 144 tiles, and prints
the resolution it settled on:

| Extent | Zoom | Tiles | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 km | 15 | 36 | 1.4 m/px |
| 25 km | 13 | 81 | 5.8 m/px |
| 100 km | 11 | 81 | 23 m/px |

4 km is unchanged, so nothing already downloaded needs fetching again.
The stitched image is capped at 4096 px because the plan view draws it
into a panel a few hundred pixels wide.

### Ground: the plane worlds were widened, the heightmap worlds were not

`tools/far_ground_apply.py` changes one number - the ground plane's
`<size>` - from 8 km to 220 km, and the ground collision box from 100 km
to match. A plane carries flat colour rather than a tiled texture, so the
width costs nothing to render and cannot tile visibly. Seven worlds took
it.

The five heightmap worlds were deliberately left alone. Stretching a
heightmap stretches its relief with it, and dropping a flat apron around
one leaves a step at the join as tall as whatever the terrain happens to
be at its own edge - Hoggar's heightmap spans 1151 m of height in 4 km, so
that step could be hundreds of metres. `--list` says which worlds can be
flown long.

## Where it stands

Tested, offline: the envelope defaults and fall-backs, that only the range
differs, that the plugin updates the station's own `LIMITS` dict, the
zoom-and-tile arithmetic at six extents and three providers, that all
seven plane worlds were widened and all twelve still parse.
`tools/test_range_profile.py`, 38 checks.

Never run against Gazebo or PX4. Nobody has yet watched a widened world
load, flown past 4 km from home, or fetched imagery at 100 km. The things
most likely to be wrong are therefore: whether a 220 km plane looks right
at the horizon, and whether PX4's EKF is content 100 km from its origin -
the flat-earth geodesy in the station is good to a few parts in a thousand
at that distance, which has never mattered at 500 m.

Also unchanged and worth saying plainly: scenery is only ever near home.
A 100 km leg is a long leg over an empty plane.
