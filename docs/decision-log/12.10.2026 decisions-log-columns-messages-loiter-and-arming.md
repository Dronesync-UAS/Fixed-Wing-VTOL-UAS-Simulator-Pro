# 12.10.2026 - Two columns that fit, a message panel, LOITER nose-first,
# and an aircraft that looks armed

Seven things, all from flying it and looking at it rather than from
reading the code.

## The rails do not scroll any more

**The complaint:** "the scroll up and down in the side columns needs to
go as it covers some of the functions."

It was right, and worse than it sounds. The scrollbar floated over the
RIGHT-HAND END of every control in the rail - which is exactly where the
number boxes and the drop-downs are. A control a mouse cannot reach
might as well not be there.

So the rails are fixed: `overflow: hidden`, no scrollbar, everything on
screen at once. That turns the rail into a budget rather than a shelf,
and `tools/test_console_browser.py` now fails the build if either rail
overflows at 1280x720 - the smallest screen this is taught on. If a new
control will not fit, something has to move into the settings window.
That is the trade, made on purpose.

Fitting it took four changes, in order of how much they saved:

* **Labels beside the number boxes, not above them.** Six numbers on
  their own lines was seventy pixels of label. A drop-down keeps the
  full width, because its options are words.
* **Climb / descend moved to the right column.** The left was eight
  pixels too tall and this was the cleanest eight pixels.
* **The spinner arrows are gone from the number boxes.** They ate
  fourteen pixels and then clipped the third digit - "100" was coming
  out as "10C", which was visible on screen and invisible to every test
  until one was written for it. Nobody sets a cruise height by clicking
  an arrow eleven times.
* **A short-screen mode below 800 px tall**: smaller buttons, tighter
  gaps, and the message panel gives up its heading.

## Two columns, arranged as a GCS

    LEFT - make it fly            RIGHT - change or end what it is doing
      LAUNCH                        RECOVER
        ARM                           HOLD
        Climb (m) / TAKEOFF           Return to launch
      MANOEUVRE                       LAND
        Hover (MC)                    DISARM
        Transition / Cruise         ADJUST
          / Forward (FW)              Height (m) / Climb / descend
        Radius / Speed / Nose       FLIGHT MODE
          / LOITER                    Position, Manual
                                    EMERGENCY
                                      TERMINATE
                                    MESSAGE

HOLD, RETURN, LAND and DISARM are at the TOP of the right column, in a
fixed place, and nothing can push them down. They are the four a student
reaches for without thinking.

Each control that needs numbers is now one block with a faint
background, so the numbers obviously belong to the button under them
rather than floating between two.

Set home and Freeze left the rails entirely - neither is a flying
control. They are in Settings > Session.

## The message panel

**The complaint:** "when messages pop up they dont disappear ... they
appear are in corners and not easy to see at glance."

Both halves were true. The old box floated over a corner of the picture,
and an ALERT never faded at all - so by the end of a lesson the console
was showing something that had happened twenty minutes earlier, over the
top of the view.

It is now a panel at the foot of the right column, under TERMINATE,
filling the space nothing else needs. Always the same place, always the
same shape, at eye level in the column the instructor is already
working in.

* **Three states, three colours.** Grey and dim when there is nothing to
  say, green for an ordinary reply, red for an alert. An alert pulses
  four times so it catches the eye, then settles - a panel that pulses
  for ever stops being a signal.
* **Everything clears itself.** An ordinary reply after thirty seconds,
  an alert after two minutes. Long enough to walk back to the screen,
  short enough that it is never stale.
* **It says how long ago.** "just now", "40 s ago", "3 min ago". An
  instructor who looks away and back needs to know whether the panel is
  reporting now or a minute ago.

## LOITER flies nose-first

**The complaint:** "correct loiter as it still flies sideways in a
circle. loiter mean a circle with the aircraft nose heading forward no
sideways."

The cause was this project's own default. It sent ORBIT_YAW_BEHAVIOUR 0,
HOLD FRONT TO CIRCLE CENTER - MAVLink's default, and the right answer
when the camera is the point of the exercise, because the aircraft keeps
looking at the thing it is circling. It is not what "loiter" means to a
pilot.

The default is now 3, HOLD FRONT TANGENT TO CIRCLE, which PX4 implements
in `FlightTaskOrbit.cpp` lines 350-354 - the nose on the tangent with the
yaw rate fed forward, so it turns at exactly the rate the circle needs
instead of chasing it. Confirmed against the v1.15.4 source and against
`OrbitStatus.msg`, where that behaviour really is 3.

Pointing at the centre is still offered. It is just not the default.

Worth knowing: `FlightTaskOrbit` is a MULTICOPTER task. The crabbing was
so visible because the aircraft was in hover, where it CAN fly sideways.
A wing cannot, so in fixed-wing flight PX4 does not use this task at all.

## The chase view shows some sky

**The complaint:** "most of the view is the terrain."

Arithmetic, not taste. The camera sat 2.2 m above the aircraft pitched
0.24 rad down. The vertical half-angle of that lens at 16:9 is
`atan(tan(0.525) * 9/16) = 0.31 rad`, so a 0.24 rad down-tilt put the
horizon 77% of the way from the middle of the frame to the top. Almost
everything below it was ground.

Now 1.1 m up and 0.12 rad down: the horizon sits 39% of the way up, so
about a third of the frame is sky, and the aircraft is still within two
degrees of the centre - because raising the camera by h and tilting down
by atan(h/9) are the same angle. The boom presets moved from 13.8
degrees to 7 for the same reason and with the same result.

## Every light starts off

**The complaint:** "keep all lights off when starting as lights will be
selected during pre-flight checks."

The station's own state was already "off". The problem was Gazebo: a
`<light>` in a model file is ON the moment the world loads, because SDF
has no "off" element - only the runtime message this project sends. So
the aircraft came up with all six lights burning while the console said
they were off, which is the worst of both.

`aircraft_lights.all_off()` is now sent twenty-five seconds after
start-up, once Gazebo is listening. And there is a new pre-flight
checklist item, "Aircraft lights set for the conditions", because that
is where the decision belongs.

## Propellers that turn when it is armed

**The complaint:** "when arming make propellers spin faster as real
aircraft."

A real cause, found in PX4's own airframe file
(`4004_gz_standard_vtol`, v1.15.4):

    param set-default SIM_GZ_EC_MIN1 10
    param set-default SIM_GZ_EC_MAX1 1500

Those are rotor speeds in radians a second - the same units as the
model's own `<maxRotVelocity>1500</>`. So an armed aircraft sitting on
the ground spun its rotors at 10 rad/s: about 95 rpm, which on screen is
a propeller that is not moving.

`plugins/idle_spin.py` raises it to 130 rad/s, worked out rather than
picked:

    thrust = motorConstant * omega^2,  motorConstant = 2e-05
    hover needs 5 * 9.81 / 4 = 12.3 N a rotor
    omega_hover = sqrt(12.3 / 2e-05) = 784 rad/s

130 is 17% of hover speed, which is where a real machine idles, and puts
1.4 N under the aircraft in total - under 3% of its weight, so it cannot
creep or lift. The ceiling in the control is 190, because the minimum is
a floor on what the mixer can ask for and raising it too far eats the
authority to hold attitude at low throttle.

The pusher is deliberately left at 0. A fixed-wing pusher does not
windmill on the pad.

Applied over MAVLink at runtime through a new `simulator_link.set_param`,
which reads the value back to confirm it rather than assuming - a
parameter that silently clamped looks exactly like one that was set.
Nothing is written to PX4's saved parameters and nothing in PX4's tree is
edited.

The motor sound got the matching half: the rotors spool up over 1.6
seconds on arming and run down over 2.6, instead of switching on. That
ramp IS the sound of arming; a note that appears at full idle reads as a
switch being thrown.

## What has not been done

* **None of this has been flown.** The LOITER fix is read off the PX4
  source; the idle spin is arithmetic off the model and the airframe
  file; the chase angle is arithmetic off the lens. Nobody has armed the
  aircraft, watched the propellers, flown a circle, or looked at the
  chase view with any of it in place.
* **The idle spin has a real risk in it** that only a flight will
  settle: raising the output floor reduces how far the mixer can cut
  thrust on one rotor. At 17% of hover that should be nowhere near the
  limit, but "should" is not "seen". If the aircraft will not descend
  cleanly, put it back with the same control.
* **The wind direction convention** is still reasoned rather than
  measured, as `plugins/wind_gust.py` has said since 11 Oct.
