The training clearing was smaller than the operating limits (11 October 2026)

**Reported:** in `training_area` the aircraft ends up among rock spires shortly after takeoff, while
the same aircraft flies cleanly in `flat_test`.

**Not a bug, and not the terrain alignment.** Measured from the heightmap:

| From home | Highest ground, relative to home |
|---|---|
| 400 m | +0 m (the clearing) |
| 600 m | +235 m |
| 800 m | +397 m |

The clearing was flat only to about 400 m. The operating limits in `instructor_station.py` are a
**500 m range from home** and a **120 m ceiling**. So beyond 400 m there was no airspace a student
could legally use - at the 120 m ceiling the aircraft is already more than 100 m below ground in
places, and at the default ~9 m takeoff altitude it is among spires as soon as it leaves the
clearing. The world and the limits contradicted each other.

**Fixed by making the terrain match the limits, not by flying outside them.**
`tools/widen_clearing.py` carves the clearing flat to **600 m** and blends it smoothly back into
the real SRTM terrain by **1000 m** (smoothstep, so there is no cliff at either join). Result:

- highest ground anywhere within the 500 m range limit is now **+0 m** relative to home
- a 120 m ceiling gives **120 m of clearance across the whole operating area**
- the real mountains remain beyond 700 m, so the scenery and the terrain-awareness value of the
  world are kept

Climbing above the ridges instead was considered and rejected: it would need over 400 m, breaking
the 120 m ceiling the training is built around. The limits model real UK rules and are the point
of the exercise, so the terrain gives way.

Only the Hoggar heightmap is touched. The other three worlds have their own terrain and clearings
and are left alone - they need the same check before use. Both copies of the heightmap are
updated, the live one in PX4 and the repo's backup, each with a `.preclearing` backup, and
`--revert` restores them.

**Three mistakes of mine worth recording, since all three cost time:**
1. I theorised that the terrain pose offset was misaligned. Measuring the heightmap disproved it.
2. I then concluded the camera was to blame. It was, for the "invisible aircraft" in `flat_test` -
   but not here, and I repeated it instead of measuring the terrain along the actual flight path.
3. The flight path measurement was the thing that settled it, and it took one script. The pattern
   across all three: **measure the specific thing being complained about, first.**
