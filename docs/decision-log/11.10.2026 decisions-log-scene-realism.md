# Why the worlds looked like diagrams — 11 October 2026

Asked: *"can the environment view be more realistic"*. Three causes, all in
the world files, none of them to do with resolution.

## 1. There was no sky

Every world set `<background>0.6 0.75 0.9 1</background>` and nothing else:
one flat blue behind everything, with no horizon, no cloud and no change
with viewing angle.

gz-sim8 reads `<scene><sky/></scene>` — `RenderUtil.cc:1185`,
`if (scene.Sky()) scene->SetSkyEnabled(true)`. `Ogre2Scene::SetSkyEnabled`
(line 1529) loads `skybox.dds` as an environment map and, the part that
matters here, applies it to **every sensor camera** as well as the Gazebo
window, so the chase and nose feeds get it too.

**What `<sky/>` does not do:** sdformat lets you write `<time>`,
`<sunrise>`, `<clouds>` and so on inside it, and gz-rendering8 ignores all
of them. It is a fixed cubemap, on or off. Those sub-elements are
deliberately not written, because configuration that does nothing is worse
than none.

## 2. The sun was nearly overhead

`direction -0.4 0.2 -0.9` is about 63° of elevation. Light from overhead
casts almost no shadow, so terrain relief, the airframe's own shape and the
ground all read flat however sharp the texture is. Lowered to about 32°.

## 3. Ambient was 0.6

That is a very bright fill, and fill light is exactly what removes contrast:
every surface gets most of its brightness regardless of which way it faces.
Reduced to 0.40 with the sun's diffuse warmed slightly, so a slope turned
away from the sun is visibly darker than one facing it.

Shadows were already enabled in every world and were doing nothing visible
because of 2 and 3.

## This one is taste

Unlike the terrain resolution — where 6.4 pixels per metre was a defect with
a number attached — this is a judgement about how it should look.
`tools/realistic_scene.py --revert` puts every world back exactly as it was.
If the shadows turn out too long for teaching, or the ground too dark to
pick the aircraft out against, reverting costs one command.

**Not yet seen.** The reasoning says the light will model the terrain; only
a look says whether it reads better or just darker.
