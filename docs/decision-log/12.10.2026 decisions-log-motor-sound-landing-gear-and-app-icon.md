# 12.10.2026 - Motor sound, landing gear, the gimbal, and the app icon

Asked for, in one message, with five reference images: realistic motor
sound for the four VTOL rotors and a distinct sound for the forward
motor; confirmation that the navigation and strobe lights flash like a
real aircraft; on/off switches for the sound and for the lights; four
legs and a gimbal camera under the aircraft; and the desktop icon
changed to the UAV Academy logo with GCS added under ACADEMY.

## The motor sound is synthesised, not played

`motor_sound.py`. WebAudio in the browser, no sound file. Two reasons:
nothing extra to install on a teaching machine, and the note can follow
the throttle continuously instead of being a loop that announces itself
as a loop.

Each rotor is three layers - a blade-pass tone, its harmonics, and
band-passed broadband noise. The noise is most of it; the first version
without it sounded like a synthesiser, which is the whole failure mode
to avoid here. The four hover rotors are four separate voices detuned
under 2% from each other, because the slow throb of a hovering
multirotor IS the beating between rotors that are not quite in step. One
voice for all four does not produce it at all.

The pusher gets a different voice - lower fundamental, stronger upper
harmonics, and a 9x whine - so an instructor can hear, without looking,
that the forward motor has come in.

### What is real in it and what is not

Real, read out of PX4-gazebo-models `models/standard_vtol/model.sdf`:
four hover rotors at maxRotVelocity 1500 rad/s with motorConstant 2e-05,
one `rotor_puller` at 3500 rad/s with motorConstant 8.54858e-06, rotors
0 and 1 CCW and 2 and 3 CW. So the model does treat the pusher as a
different, faster motor, and that difference is honoured.

The audible frequencies are NOT derived from those numbers and are not
presented as if they were. 1500 rad/s is about 14,300 rpm and 3500 rad/s
about 33,400 rpm; they are simulation tuning values, not propeller
speeds, and taken literally the pusher would sing above a kilohertz.
Nothing in the simulator publishes a real propeller RPM, so the pitches
were chosen by ear and said to be chosen by ear.

### What drives it

`throttle_pct`, added to the telemetry this session. It is PX4's own
operator-feedback figure out of VFR_HUD - `streams/VFR_HUD.hpp` lines
92-110 fill it with `100 * max(|thrust_setpoint_0|, |thrust_setpoint_1|)`
while armed and 0.0 while disarmed. PX4's own comment there says a VTOL
has no single throttle during a transition and this is a heuristic for
operator awareness. Good enough to drive a sound; not a number to teach
from, and the code says so.

Which motors turn follows `vtol_state`: the four rotors in hover and
through BOTH transitions, the pusher in fixed wing and on the way into
it. The pusher is stopped for the back-transition. Getting that backwards
would teach a student that a VTOL keeps its lift rotors turning in
cruise, so it is the first thing the test checks.

No distance modelling, no doppler. Both camera views are on the aircraft,
so the listener is treated as being on it too.

## The lights already flashed; the switch is new

Nothing about the flash pattern changed. The navigation lights are
STEADY - red to port, green to starboard, white at the tail - because
that is what a position light is for, and a student taught otherwise
will be marked wrong. The white strobes double-flash about once a
second and the red beacon flashes while armed. `tools/test_night_ops.py`
has checked that pattern since it was built.

Two things did change. `tools/aircraft_lights_apply.py` still carried a
paragraph saying the strobes do not flash, left over from before the
flash driver existed; it now says they do, and says when that stopped
being true. And `plugins/aircraft_lights.py` now takes `on=1` / `on=0`
as well as toggling, so the settings switch and the lights cannot end up
disagreeing about which way round they are - a checkbox that only ever
says "flip it" shows the opposite of the truth the first time anything
else touches the lights.

Still never seen running in Gazebo.

## The legs are two skid frames, not four posts

This is the part the words alone would have got wrong. "Add 4 legs and a
gimbal camera under the aircraft" reads as one leg under each rotor and a
gimbal on the nose. The photographs show neither: two fore-aft rails
under the fuselage close to the centreline, four tubular legs hanging off
their ends and splayed outboard with the front pair leaning forward and
the rear pair aft, plain open tubes with no feet, and the gimbal ball
amidships between them. That is what `tools/aircraft_legs_apply.py`
builds.

Positions are arithmetic off the model's own figures - +X forward
(`rotor_puller` at x = -0.22), the belly at z = -0.095 (collision pose
-0.07, box 0.05 thick), rotors at +-0.35. The splay angles and the 0.32 m
stance are proportions off the photographs.

### Visual only, and the one compromise

The legs and the gimbal have no collision. Four thin cylinders touching
the ground is the contact case ODE handles worst, and landing gear that
makes the aircraft jitter or tip on the runway would be a worse lesson
than no landing gear.

That leaves the height. The tool therefore lowers the EXISTING belly
collision box from z = -0.07 to z = -0.295, putting its underside at the
feet. The aircraft then stands at the right height on the same single box
it has always stood on - no new contact behaviour, just a lower one.

The cost, stated plainly: a world that spawns the model at a fixed height
now drops it about 0.22 m further before it settles. That has not been
watched happen. `--no-stance` skips it, at the price of legs that clip
through the ground.

The gimbal is a body, not a mount. It does not move, and the nose camera
picture still comes from the camera it always came from. Re-aiming a
working view was not asked for.

## The icon is measured, not eyeballed

`sim/icons/uas-gcs.svg` and its stop twin. The geometry is read off the
supplied logo at the 1254 px it came at: red squares x 194-1060,
y 391-666, 272 wide with 25 px gaps; ACADEMY 802 wide with a 105 px cap
height; tile 1128 square inset 63; red #fb0100 on grey #535353 to
#39393b. The SVG uses that same 1254 viewBox so those numbers are these
numbers. The block is shifted up to make room for GCS and re-centred.

The wordmark is text, not outlines, so the file stays readable and
fixable; both words carry `textLength` with `lengthAdjust="spacing"` so
the letter spacing stretches to the measured width whatever font the
desktop has - which is also how the real logo is set.

## Tested, and not tested

`tools/test_sound_and_gear.py` (new, and in CLAUDE.md's before-committing
list) drives the sound's state machine in a real browser against fake
telemetry, checks both switches including which way round the lights one
sends, and checks the gear arithmetic - every leg top on a rail, every
leg bottom at one height, every leg leaning the right way - then applies
the tool to a copy of a standard_vtol-shaped model and reverts it.

Not tested, and nobody should assume otherwise: whether the motors SOUND
right, whether the legs and gimbal LOOK right in Gazebo, whether the
0.22 m lower stance causes anything at spawn, and whether the lights
flash on screen. All four need the simulator running and a person
watching.
