Flat test world, and the aircraft spawning inside the terrain (11 October 2026)

**Reported:** after adding the three missing Gazebo systems, the aircraft took off, drifted a
little, moved around and settled - **no uncommanded spiral and no "Attitude failure" within the
observed window**, which is the first time that has been seen since 22 August. Propellers running
at different speeds while holding position is normal: that is the controller trimming attitude in
hover, not a fault.

Not yet a confirmed fix - one takeoff, watched for under a minute. It needs repeating, and ideally
a transition to forward flight, before the 22 August issue is called closed. Record the result
here when it is.

**Separate problem found in the same flight: the aircraft spawns inside a mountain.**

From the world file, not guesswork:
- the heightmap is 4000 m x 4000 m with a height range of 0-1151 m
- the terrain model carries `<pose>-779.7 0 0 0 0 0</pose>`, shifting the whole heightmap 779.7 m
  in -X so that the carved clearing lands on the world origin
- the launcher spawns the aircraft at world `(0, 0, 615.5)`
- a 900 m x 900 m collision-only safety floor sits with its top at Z=615.5 to catch it

So the spawn point is correct only if the clearing really does land at the origin after that
-779.7 m shift. If the sign or the magnitude is wrong, the origin falls on untouched real terrain
and the aircraft starts underground - which is what was seen.

**This predates today's changes.** `tools/wind_apply.py` inserted a `<wind>` element and one plugin
before `</world>`; `tools/add_world_systems.py` inserted three plugin lines after the last existing
plugin. Neither touches geometry, pose or the heightmap. Provable on the machine:

    diff ~/PX4-Autopilot/Tools/simulation/gz/worlds/training_area.sdf.prewind \
         ~/PX4-Autopilot/Tools/simulation/gz/worlds/training_area.sdf

**Deliberately not chasing the alignment yet,** because every other test is unreadable while it
stands: a flight-control problem and a terrain problem look the same from the cockpit. Also note
the 23 August entry's two attempts at the resting-drift problem both concerned this same offset and
the heightmap's physics-versus-rendering behaviour (gazebosim/gz-sim#1295), so the alignment
question is entangled with an older open issue and deserves its own session.

**Built instead: `sim/worlds/flat_test.sdf`** - a clear, genuinely flat world for testing at real
ground level. Identical to `training_area.sdf` in everything that affects how the aircraft flies -
same physics step, gravity, magnetic field, atmosphere, spherical coordinates, the same eleven
world systems in the same order, the same wind element and WindEffects plugin. The only difference
is the ground: an infinite flat plane at Z=0 carrying the `<surface>` block copied from Gazebo's
own stock `ground_plane`, which the 23 August side-by-side test proved settles the aircraft to an
exact zero resting speed.

It needs no media files, so it drops into any PX4 checkout on its own. 100 m distance markers along
+X and +Y make drift visible by eye rather than only on instruments. Launch with
`tools/start_flat_test.sh`, which installs the world, clears a previous run, and spawns at Z=0.3.

**Framework change this forced: the world name is no longer hardcoded.** It was spelled out
separately in six files - freeze/resume, both reposition plugins, the camera topic, the
external-view helper and the wind control. A second world would have meant editing all six, and
missing one would leave a control silently talking to a world that is not running. Now in
`sim_target.py`, overridable per session:

    GCS_WORLD=flat_test python3 instructor_station.py

The model name moved with it (`GCS_MODEL`). The external-view helper is unaffected by world name -
`/gui/follow` is a GUI service, not world-scoped - which is itself worth knowing.

## Correction: the terrain alignment is right (measured, 11 October 2026)

The claim above that the clearing does not land on the origin **was wrong**. Measured from the
heightmap itself rather than reasoned from the pose offset:

- `hoggar_real_heightmap.png` is 513x513 over 4000 m, i.e. 7.81 m per pixel.
- The carved clearing is the plateau of constant value 134/255, 10,245 pixels, about a 446 m
  equivalent radius - matching the "~400m-radius flat clearing" of the 22 August entry.
- With the terrain model's `<pose>-779.7 0 0>`, that clearing's centre sits at world
  **east -149 m, north +/-54 m**, so the spawn at the world origin is about **159 m from the
  centre of a 446 m-radius clearing - comfortably inside it**.
- The heightmap value at the world origin is 134/255 = **604.8 m**, the clearing floor. The spawn
  altitude is 615.5 m, so the aircraft starts **10.7 m above flat ground** - which is exactly the
  "watch it fall under real physics and settle" behaviour the 22 August entry describes.

So the aircraft does not spawn inside a mountain. What was almost certainly seen is the **Gazebo
camera** sitting inside terrain and filling the view with rock - the same camera-placement problem
that, in `flat_test`, hid an aircraft that was demonstrably sitting on the ground at
(0, 0, 0.095). That one was settled by querying the model pose rather than by looking, and this
should have been too.

**Lesson worth keeping: look at the data before theorising from a parameter.** The -779.7 m offset
looked like a plausible culprit and the reasoning was tidy, but one measurement of the heightmap
disproved it in a minute.

The flat test world keeps its value regardless - it removes terrain as a variable, and it is where
the clean flight was achieved - but "fix the terrain alignment" should come off the list. If the
training world still looks wrong, the thing to check is the camera, with
`gz service -s /gui/move_to`.
