# The painted sky, and why the ambient light was the real problem

12 October 2026.

## What was said

> "horrible scene"
> "absolutly horrible"

Two screenshots, both of `training_area_coast`, both after the PBR
materials and the ground textures had been applied and installed and
confirmed identical in PX4's own copy of the worlds. The work had reached
Gazebo. It had changed nothing anybody could see.

## What the screenshots actually showed

Measured, not guessed:

* **The sky filled the top half of the frame and was near-white**, with no
  gradient and no cloud.
* **The horizon was a hard line** where the ground stopped.
* **Every surface was flat** - the sea read as navy paint, the grass as
  one green wash, the aircraft as a grey blob.

## The three findings

**1. Gazebo Harmonic's sky is one bit.** gz-sim8
`src/rendering/RenderUtil.cc` reads exactly three things out of
`<scene>`: `Ambient()`, `Background()`, and `Sky()` - and for the sky it
calls `SetSkyEnabled(true)` and nothing more. There is no sky material,
no gradient and no cloud layer to configure. `<sky></sky>` is the whole
feature.

**2. There is no fog in this renderer.** The word does not appear in
`RenderUtil.cc` at all. sdformat accepts `<scene><fog>`; gz-sim8 ignores
it. So the hard horizon cannot be softened the way every flight
simulator softens it, and anything that claims otherwise in this project
would be wrong.

**3. The ambient light was the reason the PBR work was invisible.** Every
world carried `<ambient>0.40 0.41 0.44</ambient>`. Forty percent flat
light from every direction at once is a lot: a normal map has no
direction to shade against, and roughness has no highlight to sharpen.
The morning's materials were real and were being washed out by a number
set weeks earlier.

Finding 3 is the uncomfortable one. The PBR commit was not wrong, and it
was not enough on its own, and nothing in the checks could have said so
- they test what is in the file, and the file was right.

## What was built

A **painted dome**: an inside-out mesh at 7 km radius, inside the
cameras' 8 km clip plane, carrying a 2048x1024 equirectangular sky image.

* `tools/make_sky_dome.py` writes the mesh and the image.
* `tools/sky_dome_apply.py` puts the dome in every world, takes
  `<sky></sky>` out, and drops the ambient to `0.26 0.28 0.32`.
* `tools/test_sky.py` is 30 offline checks.

### Why a mesh rather than an SDF `<sphere>`

Seen from inside, a sphere shows its back faces, and back faces are
culled. SDF has `<material><double_sided>`, and sdformat13 does read it,
but in gz-sim8 it only reaches Ogre through `SetAlphaFromTexture` -
`SceneManager.cc` line 925 - which is only called when an albedo map is
present. Depending on that chain for whether the sky exists at all is a
bad trade when an inside-out mesh has no back faces to cull. Every
triangle's winding AND its normal point at the middle of the dome, and
`test_sky.py` checks both.

### Why emissive, with a black albedo

A sky is not a surface the sun falls on. The sky image is the dome's
`<emissive_map>` and its albedo is black, so it shows exactly what was
painted - no sun angle, no ambient, no half-lit dome.

### How night still works

Making the sky emissive would have left it bright blue at midnight, which
would have broken the night-operations exercise. gz-sim8's
`UserCommands.cc` line 636 subscribes `/world/NAME/material_color`, and
`VisualCommand::Execute` finds the entity by scoped name, so
`plugins/time_of_day.py` now dims the dome with the sun: 1.00 at midday,
0.42 at dusk, 0.07 at night. The note in that plugin saying the sky
could not be changed while the world ran was wrong, and has been
corrected.

## What is painted, and what is a compromise

* A zenith-to-horizon gradient, deep blue to pale.
* Cloud as periodic value noise, four octaves, wrapping in azimuth.
  It **fades out above 65 degrees of elevation**, because an
  equirectangular image pinches at the pole and stretched cloud there
  looks worse than clear sky. Look straight up and there is no cloud.
* A haze band at the horizon. This is the fog substitute: it does not
  attenuate anything, it just means the ground stops against haze
  instead of against white.
* A broad, weak sun glow where the worlds put the sun - deliberately not
  a disc, because `time_of_day` moves the sun and a painted disc would
  then be in the wrong place.

## The limit, stated plainly

**The dome is static and centred on the world origin.** Beyond about 7 km
from launch the aircraft flies out of it and the sky reverts to the
background colour. That covers the 500 m student envelope entirely. On a
100 km instructor leg, the sky will go flat. Making it follow the
aircraft is possible over the same `material_color`-style interface and
has not been done.

## What has not been tested

Nothing here has been seen in Gazebo. `tools/test_sky.py` checks the
mesh's geometry, winding, normals, UV coverage and seam; the image's
gradient, haze band, cloud variation and wrap; every world's dome, sky
removal, ambient and dome scale; and the day/night tints. It cannot check
that the dome renders, that the emissive map is honoured, or that
`material_color` reaches it. Those need eyes on a running simulator.
