12 August 2026 - SBUS-to-USB Adapter — Build Research & Parts List 

Follow-up research to the earlier roadmap entry (student/instructor app & RC controller research, same date). This entry covers the outcome of sourcing a ready-made product versus a DIY build, and the confirmed parts list for the recommended solution: converting the Skydroid H16 / Herelink's SBUS output into a USB joystick signal PX4/QGroundControl can read directly.

Finding: no ready-made retail product exists for this specific need. Searched Amazon UK, specialist RC/FPV retailers, and maker marketplaces (Tindie). The commercial market splits into two directions that don't cover this case: (1) wireless dongles that bind directly to one specific radio manufacturer's protocol (Futaba S-FHSS, FrSky ACCST, etc.) — not applicable, since Skydroid/Herelink don't use these; (2) full flight-controller boards (Pixhawk-class hardware) — massive overkill for reading a joystick signal only. The one purpose-built commercial product found (PhatDadRacing "RC USB Adapter" on Tindie, Australia) was out of stock and didn't clearly confirm SBUS support. Conclusion: this needs to be built, not bought finished — it is a well-documented DIY niche, not a manufactured product category.

Recommended build: Arduino Pro Micro (ATmega32U4) based SBUS-to-USB-joystick converter, following an established open-source project (multiple independent implementations found, e.g. froqstar/SBUSJoystick and Gerifield/sbusjoy on GitHub — free, ready-made code, no coding required beyond uploading a file). Pro Micro chosen over the alternative STM32 "Blue Pill" board because it has USB built in, needing no separate programmer tool — fewer parts, simpler build.

Parts list (confirmed):

Arduino Pro Micro board (~£6–10) — available on Amazon UK, multiple sellers.
SBUS signal inverter (~£2–5) — required because the SBUS signal is electrically inverted relative to what the board reads natively.
Jumper wires (~£3–5) — connect the receiver's SBUS/power/ground pins to the board.
USB cable — often included with the board.
(Optional) A connector matching the Skydroid receiver's SBUS port, so the adapter can be plugged in without permanently altering the receiver's wiring (~£2–5).
(Optional) Small enclosure for durability in regular classroom use (~£3–5).

Total estimated cost: ~£15–30.

Correction made during this research (documented for future reference): a "skip the inverter" wiring trick mentioned in one build guide was initially flagged as a possible simplification, but on closer reading it is specific to modifying a solder point inside certain FrSky-brand receivers — it does not apply generally, and has no confirmed applicability to the Skydroid receiver. Reverted to the standard build with a dedicated inverter component (item 2 above) rather than relying on an unconfirmed shortcut.

How the build works, summary: Skydroid SBUS wire → inverter component → three wires (signal/power/ground) into specific Arduino Pro Micro pins → one-time upload of the free open-source program via USB → board then plugs into the laptop permanently, appearing as a standard USB joystick that QGroundControl/PX4 can read.

### Open issue: VTOL attitude instability on takeoff (NOT related to the terrain work above)

**Symptom:** the standard_vtol aircraft arms and takes off normally, climbs and holds
briefly (varies 3-10 seconds observed), then enters an uncommanded spiral/climb with
repeated "Attitude failure (roll)" / "Attitude failure (pitch)" warnings and automatic
RTL/failsafe activation.

**Ruled out through direct testing tonight:**
- Not caused by the custom terrain or world file - identical failure reproduced on the
  completely stock, unmodified PX4 default world.
- Not a machine performance/timing issue - failure reproduced identically with all other
  applications closed.
- Not caused by leftover/corrupted saved parameters - failure persisted after resetting
  `parameters.bson` to a fresh default state.
- Not a general PX4/Gazebo installation problem - a plain quadcopter (`gz_x500`) on the
  stock world armed, took off, and flew perfectly with no issues.

**Conclusion:** the issue is specific to the standard_vtol aircraft model's flight
control behaviour on this PX4 build/Gazebo Harmonic pairing, most likely related to
VTOL transition or airspeed-estimation logic (the "Airspeed selector module down"
warning was observed persisting longer than usual in the failing runs). This is a
flight-control software question, separate from any terrain or environment work.
