Choosing a training environment — three of the four worlds were already suitable (11 October 2026)

**Confirmed first:** the aircraft flies correctly in the flat test world, with the three added
Gazebo systems and the corrected magnetic field. The instability is terrain-related in the sense
that the aircraft was flying into terrain, not that the flight controller was unstable.

**The constraint that decides the environment**, which had not been written down anywhere:
`instructor_station.py` enforces a **500 m range from home** and a **120 m ceiling**. A training
world therefore has to be flyable inside a 1 km circle with 120 m of vertical. Measured from each
world's own heightmap, height range within 500 m of its spawn:

| World | Place | Relief within 500 m | Within 1 km | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `training_area_blackrock` | Black Rock playa, Nevada | **0.6 m** | 0.9 m | flattest; best for handling |
| `training_area_nullarbor` | Nullarbor Plain, Australia | 7.9 m | 8.1 m | gently undulating |
| `training_area_uyuni` | Salar de Uyuni, Bolivia | 9.5 m | 9.5 m | gently undulating |
| `training_area` | Hoggar Mountains, Algeria | 0 m after widening | **582 m** | flat only inside the clearing |
| `flat_test` | none | 0 m | 0 m | no scenery; for isolating faults |

**Three real-world, naturally flat training environments already existed** and had not been used.
Black Rock in particular is a dry lake bed with 60 cm of relief across the entire operating area -
it is real terrain, real coordinates, correct magnetic field, and needs no carving at all. The
session spent its time fixing the one world that fights the limits, when three that satisfy them
were already in the folder.

**Why they would have failed if simply tried:** each world's ground sits at a different height -
Hoggar's clearing at 605 m, Black Rock's playa at 1 m - and `start_training_area.sh` hard-codes a
615.5 m spawn. Launching a sea-level world with that pose drops the aircraft 600 m. That is why
`tools/start_world.sh` exists: one launcher, the right spawn height per world (measured from each
heightmap), leftover-process cleanup, and it prints the matching `GCS_WORLD=` line for the station.

**Recommended use:**
- **Black Rock** for handling, failure injection and assessment - the flattest, with nothing for a
  student to fly into and nothing to confuse a fault diagnosis.
- **Nullarbor or Uyuni** for variety, both still comfortably inside the limits.
- **Hoggar** for terrain-awareness exercises specifically, flown inside its 600 m clearing, with
  the mountains as a visible reason to respect the range limit.
- **flat_test** kept as the diagnostic world: when something behaves oddly, fly it there first to
  rule terrain out.

**Open question for the Head of Training, not a code decision:** whether the 120 m ceiling should
be height above the home point (as now) or above ground level. On flat worlds the two are the
same, which is another argument for using them. On Hoggar they differ by hundreds of metres and
the distinction stops being academic.

## Environment selector in the station (same day)

Requested so the environment can be chosen without dropping to a terminal. It is a panel in
Simulation Control rather than a sidebar tab - the sidebar's tabs are a navigation system that
does not exist yet, and building one to hold a single dropdown would be the wrong order. Worth
revisiting when FLY / PLAN / PAYLOAD / LOGS are actually built.

Pressing **Load Environment** stops PX4 and Gazebo, relaunches them in the chosen world at that
world's own spawn height, repoints the station's Gazebo controls, re-subscribes the camera feed,
clears the terrain backdrop cache, and remembers the choice for next start-up.

**Each world is listed with its relief within the 500 m range limit**, because that is the number
that decides whether a world can be flown at all under the 120 m ceiling. An instructor choosing
an environment should see the figure that makes Hoggar different from Black Rock, rather than
discovering it in the air.

**What this needed underneath**, and is worth knowing if anything else is added later: the world
name used to be bound at import time in several modules, so changing it at runtime changed
nothing. Everything that talks to Gazebo now resolves the world at call time, through
`sim_target.service()` or `sim_target.camera_topic()`. The one thing that cannot work that way is
the camera, which holds a live subscription, so `video_feed.resubscribe()` is called explicitly on
a switch.

**Known limits, stated rather than hidden:**
- The simulator takes 15-30 seconds to come up. Telemetry returns on its own once PX4 is listening;
  if it does not, the station needs restarting.
- The launcher's output goes to `~/uas_sim_logs/sim_launch.log`, not the station window, so a
  failed launch is quiet - check that file if an environment does not come up.
- `GCS_WORLD` in the environment still overrides the remembered choice, which is what makes a
  one-off session possible without changing the saved setting.
