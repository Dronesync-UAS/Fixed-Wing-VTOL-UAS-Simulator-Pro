# 14 Oct 2026 - What the dark ground actually is

Three attempts between 12 and 14 Oct changed the heightmap, the material
and the mesh, and none of them moved the picture. This entry is the
measurement that explains why, and it is not what any of them assumed.

## Two real findings, both measured

### 1. `<geometry><plane>` ignores its SDF material in this build

The 220 km ground in the seven flat worlds was a `<plane>`. Its material
was never rendered - the ground drew a default grey-brown and no
`<material>` written anywhere could change it.

Measured on the coast world, everything running:

| what was changed | what the nose camera saw |
|---|---|
| ground material set to magenta, geometry left a `<plane>` | (151.8, 137.5, 126.5) - **no change at all** |
| the same magenta material, geometry changed to a `<box>` | (160.1, 2.1, 158.6) - magenta |

That single pair explains every earlier failure. The flat heightmap, the
explicit material, the 8 km mesh and the normal maps were all downstream
of a surface that was never going to show any of them. It also explains
why the five HEIGHTMAP worlds looked right the whole time: a heightmap is
not a plane, so its material and texture were always honoured.

`tools/far_ground_apply.py --all --solid` turns the ground visual into a
box 2 m thick, sunk so its top face is exactly where the plane's surface
was. Nothing else in the world moves and the collision is untouched - it
has always been a separate sunken box.

### 2. Textures do not render on anything except a heightmap

Worth knowing before anyone tries to put imagery on the ground again.
Tried on 14 Oct, all on the coast world:

* `emissive_map` on a box - no texture
* `emissive_map` on an OBJ mesh carrying proper UVs - no texture
* the satellite image resized to a power of two (2048x2048) - no texture

And the measurement that settles it: **the sky dome does not render its
texture either.** The sky region of the camera has a spread of **0.00** -
flat to the last pixel - while `sky_day.png` itself has a spread of 46.96.
What we have been calling the painted sky is the scene `<background>`.

So the 13 Oct note saying "`<emissive_map>`, same file - renders the
texture" is **wrong**, and anything built on it needs re-checking. In this
Gazebo Harmonic / ogre2 build the only surface that renders a texture is
`<heightmap><texture>`.

## And the thing that is actually filling the camera

Not the ground. Removing `ground_near` changed nothing. Sinking the far
ground changed nothing. Removing BOTH changed nothing.

Removing the 60 **scenery** models changed it at once: (151.8, 137.5,
126.5) became (140.4, 141.3, 108.3) - green-dominant, and close to the
dune grass the world asks for.

So what fills the lower half of every camera at the coast spawn is the
scenery's own large flat-coloured slabs - a 2600 m sea ellipsoid and
3000 m beach boxes, all sitting at z = 0.025 to 0.03, just above the
ground. They are intended content. They are also big, flat and
untextured, which is what reads as "the dark ground".

## What this entry does NOT claim

**The dark ground is not fixed.** The plane-to-box change is correct and
necessary - a ground whose material is ignored can never be fixed by
anything - but on its own it is invisible, because the scenery covers it.

Making it look right means giving the scenery slabs better materials, or
fewer and smaller ones. That is a content decision about what each
training area should look like, not a rendering bug, and it should be
taken deliberately rather than by someone guessing at a world file at
two in the morning.

## The standing lesson, again

The first question was never "which material should the ground have". It
was "which surface am I actually looking at", and nobody had answered it.
Colouring each candidate a different colour and taking one photograph
answered in one restart what three days of material edits could not.

Related: `13.10.2026 decisions-log-the-ground-that-had-no-texture.md`,
whose conclusion that `ground_near` is invisible was right, and whose
