# Visibility and precipitation — what Gazebo Harmonic actually offers (11 October 2026)

## The question this closes

The original task, back at the start of this session, was to wire a
`weather_proxy.py` draft: wind, visibility and precipitation injection,
"same pattern as the GPS proxy". Two things turned out to be wrong with that
premise, and both are now settled rather than suspected.

**The draft does not exist.** Searched the Getac for anything matching
`*weather*`, plus Google Drive and Dropbox. Nothing. `gps_dropout_proxy.py`
is real (5.2 KB, 10 Aug, in `~/UAS-Academy/Archive/instructor_panel/`);
`weather_proxy.py` never was.

**The GPS proxy is the wrong pattern to copy.** Reading it settles that. It
sits between PX4 and QGroundControl and rewrites `GPS_RAW_INT` so
QGroundControl *believes* GPS is lost — satellites to 0, fix type 0, eph/epv
to 65535. The aircraft still has GPS and still navigates on it. Copying that
for wind would paint wind on the instruments while the aircraft flew in
still air, which is worse than not having it. Wind already goes into Gazebo
for real and actually pushes the airframe.

Also worth recording, since someone will rely on it: that proxy is one-way.
`mav_in` listens on 14555, `mav_out` sends to 14550, and there is no reverse
path, so QGroundControl can display through it but cannot command the
aircraft.

## What exists in Harmonic, checked rather than recalled

**Fog: nothing.** gz-rendering8's `Scene.hh` contains zero occurrences of
"fog". gz-sim8's `RenderUtil.cc` — 3904 lines — contains zero. There is no
fog API to call and no SDF fog that gets applied. This is not "hard", it is
absent.

**Particles: a real, runtime-controllable system.**
`gz::sim::systems::ParticleEmitter` subscribes per emitter, on the
`<topic>` the emitter declares or
`/model/{model}/link/{link}/particle_emitter/{emitter}/cmd` otherwise, and
takes `gz.msgs.ParticleEmitter`. `SceneManager.cc` lines 1610–1663 apply
`rate`, `duration`, `emitting`, `particle_size`, `lifetime`,
`min_velocity`+`max_velocity` (both or neither) and `scale_rate` at runtime.
`particle_scatter_ratio` is not in that list, so it is SDF-only.

And the telling detail: gz-sim8's own `examples/worlds/particle_emitter.sdf`
names one of its emitters **`fog_generator`**. Particles are not a
workaround for the missing fog API — upstream, they are the mechanism.

## Two values that are not guesses

**Emission direction is the emitter's local +X.**
`Ogre2ParticleEmitter::CreateParticleSystem()` calls
`setDirection(Ogre::Vector3::UNIT_X)` and nothing in gz-rendering8 ever
changes it. Rain therefore needs its emitter pitched 90° about Y, mapping
local +X onto world −Z. Without that, rain falls sideways. This is the kind
of thing that looks like a bug for an hour.

**Ogre holds 5000 particles per emitter** (`setParticleQuota(5000)`, same
function). Particles alive at once is roughly rate × lifetime, so fog's 25 s
lifetime caps it near 200/s and rain's 11 s near 450/s. Past the quota Ogre
silently stops emitting and reports nothing. Every intensity the station
offers stays under it, and `tools/test_weather.py` asserts that rather than
trusting it — the worst rain setting sits at 3300 of 5000.

## What was built

`tools/weather_apply.py` adds the ParticleEmitter system and a static
`weather_cell` model, holding a fog emitter and a rain emitter, to all five
training worlds. Both start `<emitting>false</emitting>`, so an untouched
sim is unchanged — the rule wind followed. The cell is placed at each
world's own ground height from `sim_target`, because a fixed Z would bury it
600 m underground in Hoggar. Same safety as `wind_apply.py`: backup to
`.preweather`, parse the result, auto-restore anything that breaks,
`--dry-run` and `--revert`.

`plugins/weather.py` adds **Set Weather** under Experimental: Clear, Haze,
Fog, Rain, Heavy rain, Fog and rain.

## The thing that must not be misrepresented

**This changes what the camera sees. It does not change how the aircraft
flies.** Particles are rendering; nothing in gz-sim8 couples them to
aerodynamics. Wind is the only weather in this simulator that touches the
flight model.

That is a real training scenario — fly into a cell and lose sight of the
aircraft on camera — and it is the honest half of what was asked for. Every
non-clear condition the control returns says so in its own message, and
`tools/test_weather.py` asserts that the caveat is present, so it cannot be
quietly dropped in a later edit. It must not be logged or taught as flying
in weather.

## Testing

`tools/test_weather.py` stubs `gz` and inspects the exact payloads: every
condition addresses *both* emitters (otherwise rain keeps falling inside
"Fog" — the obvious silent bug), unused emitters are explicitly switched
off, every rate stays under the quota, the status light follows the
condition and is reset by a world change, an unknown condition is refused
and publishes nothing, a failed publish is reported rather than swallowed,
and the flight-model caveat is present on every condition. All pass.

`tools/weather_apply.py` was run against the repository's own world copies:
all five parsed, the structure was asserted (one plugin, one model, two
emitters, both off, rain pitched 90°), `tools/check_worlds.py` reported
5 worlds and 0 problems, a re-run skipped all five, and `--revert` restored
them exactly.

## First live look, same day — and the bug it found

Run on the machine: both topics appeared, the control worked, weather
visibly changed. And: *"heavy rain is not much rain seen falling."*

Correct, and the cause is a field this file got wrong.

**Particle size is (width, height). Z is discarded.**
`Ogre2ParticleEmitter` passes `particleSize[0]` and `particleSize[1]` to
`setDefaultDimensions()` and never reads `[2]`. Rain was written
`0.08 0.08 0.70` — the streak length put in Z, where it was silently thrown
away, leaving 8 cm squares. An ignored field does not warn; it just renders
something that looks like the feature not working.

**And the rates were about a tenth of what the budget allowed.** Rain at
220/s with an 11 s lifetime used 2420 of the 5000 quota, spread over a
140 x 140 m box from 95 m up: 0.017 particles per square metre. Barely
anything, exactly as observed.

Retuned, with the arithmetic rather than by eye:

| | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| rain particle size | `0.08 0.08 0.70` → 8 cm squares | `0.12 2.0 0` → 2 m streaks |
| rain spawn height | 95 m | 45 m (shorter fall → shorter lifetime → higher rate ceiling) |
| rain box | 140 x 140 m | 70 x 70 m |
| rain lifetime | 11 s | 4 s |
| heaviest rain | 220/s, 48% of quota | 1150/s, 92% of quota |
| rain density | 0.017 /m² | 0.94 /m² |
| fog box | 240 x 240 m | 120 x 120 m |
| fog rate | 14/s, 7% of quota | 160/s, 80% of quota |
| fog coverage | ~1x | 90x over |

`tools/test_weather.py` now guards all of it from the world file itself:
that both particle dimensions are non-zero and nothing is hidden in the
ignored Z, that rain is a streak rather than a square, that rain reaches the
ground inside its lifetime, and that full intensity actually spends the
particle budget (the original fault) rather than a tenth of it. Fog and rain
are measured differently on purpose — fog has to obscure, so billboard
coverage is the number; rain has to be seen falling without becoming a wall,
so streaks per square metre is.

**Still not confirmed:** whether the retuned settings look right. The
numbers now say they should; only a look will say they do.
