Audit of every instructor command (11 October 2026)

Prompted by "the commands do not respond properly". Every one of the 30 controls was called with
the MAVLink and Gazebo layers stubbed, recording what each actually sends, and compared with what
its button claims to do.

**The reported symptom was not a command fault.** Two instructor stations were running: the second
could not take port 8080, threw inside its server thread, and the main process carried on printing
"Connected." The browser kept talking to the first station - old code, old world - so its buttons
behaved oddly for no visible reason. The station now refuses to start in that situation and says
so. Worth stating plainly, because it had already caused one earlier confusion the same day.

**Correct, verified by the audit.** All MAVLink command IDs match their labels: ARM/DISARM 400,
TAKEOFF 22, LAND 21, RTL 20, LOITER 34 (DO_ORBIT), VTOL transitions 3000, SET_HOME 179, motor
tests 310 (ACTUATOR_TEST, each preceded by its parameter set). All Gazebo controls now resolve the
world at call time and followed a switch to Black Rock correctly.

**Defect found and fixed: Reset to Spawn Point and Reset Scenario used a fixed pose.** Both carried
`SPAWN_POSE = "0,0,615.5,0,0,0"` - Hoggar's old spawn height. On Black Rock, whose playa is at
1.3 m, pressing either would have teleported the aircraft to 615 m. On Hoggar itself it was 10 m
wrong after the safety slab was removed. The height now comes from the environment list, the same
source the launcher uses, and an unknown world gives a clear refusal rather than a wrong number.

**Not a defect, but worth knowing before an instructor is told these work:** four of the five
failure-injection buttons - Data Link (C2) Loss, Engine Failure, RC Signal Loss, Motor Failure -
are built on `MAV_CMD_INJECT_FAILURE` (420). This project's own 10 August entry records that
command as "accepted and acknowledged but has no visible effect in QGroundControl - a known,
unfixed PX4 bug", which is exactly why the GPS dropout proxy was written instead of using it for
GPS. The same doubt therefore applies to these four, and none of them has a confirmed-working
entry in this log.

They may work - the 23 August entry describes a live test of motor and engine failure that
produced recoverable behaviour, which suggests at least those two do something - but "appears to
work" and "verified" are different, and CAP722B assessment should not rest on the difference.
**Each of the four needs one live test with a stated expected outcome, and the result recorded
here.** Until then they should not be presented to instructors as confirmed.

**Also unverified: Toggle GPS Failure** depends on
`~/UAS-Academy/Archive/instructor_panel/gps_dropout_proxy.py` still existing at that path. The
plugin starts it as a background script and reports success on launch, not on effect, so a moved
or missing file would look like a working button that does nothing.

## Why "nothing happens" was invisible, and the three fixes that followed

A direct test settled it: `commander takeoff` typed at the PX4 console took off cleanly and flew
stably, while the station's TAKEOFF button did nothing. PX4 was fine; the station was not.

**No command checked whether PX4 accepted it.** `grep -l send_command_and_wait plugins/*.py`
returned nothing - not one plugin used the acknowledgement helper that already existed in
`simulator_link.py`. Every button fired its command and returned a success message regardless, so
a command PX4 refused was indistinguishable from one it obeyed. That is why the status line never
told us anything across a whole afternoon of asking for it: it could not. TAKEOFF, LAND, RETURN TO
LAUNCH and LOITER now report what PX4 actually said - accepted, refused with the reason, or no
acknowledgement at all - and TAKEOFF adds the likely cause when the aircraft is disarmed, since a
blocked arm is what makes a takeoff silently do nothing.

This is the most useful change of the session. It does not fix a single command; it makes every
future failure state its own cause instead of presenting as a dead button.

**The camera subscription was made before the simulator existed.** Switching environment
re-subscribed immediately, but the simulator takes 15-30 seconds to come up, so the subscription
pointed at a topic nothing was publishing on yet. A watchdog now re-subscribes when no frames have
arrived for 8 seconds or when the topic no longer matches the current world.

**Panels stretched to the height of the tallest in their row.** The grid had `align-content: start`
but not `align-items: start`, so every panel in a row grew to match its tallest neighbour -
which is what kept forcing scrolling and manual resizing after a fit.

**Still open and not to be assumed fixed:** the EKF warnings in the PX4 log -
`Preflight Fail: Yaw estimate error` and `Preflight: GPS Vertical Pos Drift too high` - appear in
every world and are what blocks arming. They are the reason takeoff was refused in the first place.
The corrected magnetic field did not clear them. That is the next thing to chase, and it is a PX4
estimator question rather than a station one.

## The erratic flight was wind, left switched on

Reported: the aircraft lifts about a metre, moves forward on its own, and hits the ground
repeatedly. The station's status line read "Taking off - climbing 20 m above the current
position", so PX4 accepted the command - the acknowledgement fix doing its job.

**The Wind On / Off control was lit green in the screenshot: 8 m/s from 270 degrees.** A
standard_vtol hovering a metre off the ground in an 8 m/s crosswind is pushed sideways into the
ground. That is the behaviour, and it is also a plausible explanation for "flies backward in a
straight line" after LOITER and for the earlier erratic flights, all of which happened with that
light on.

**This is a flaw in the wind control as I built it.** The on/off state lived only in the station,
while the wind itself lives in the Gazebo world. Restarting the simulator or switching environment
loads a world whose `<wind>` is zero, but the button kept showing "on" - so the station's light and
the simulator disagreed, and an instructor reading the console could believe wind was off when it
was not, or believe it was on when it was not.

Fixed with a small registry in `sim_target`: anything holding state tied to the running world
registers a reset callback, and the environment control calls `world_changed()` after a switch.
The wind control registers its own reset, so a new world starts calm with its light out.

**Lesson, and it is the same one as the rest of the day:** the console showed a state the simulator
did not have. Every serious confusion today has come from the station asserting something it had
not checked - commands reported as sent when PX4 refused them, a failure light lit when PX4 had
rejected the injection, a wind light on while the world was calm. The fix in each case was to make
the console report what is true rather than what was intended.

**Still to confirm by flight:** whether turning wind off gives a clean takeoff. If it does, the
22 August instability may have the same explanation for at least some of its occurrences, though
that control did not exist in August and cannot account for the original reports.
